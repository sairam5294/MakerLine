# Must catch (1 point each)
1. Real competitors, not "big suites only": clinic booking tools already popular with small Canadian clinics (e.g. Jane App, plus others such as Cliniko, ClinicSense, Noterro or Square Appointments) — "simpler" must pass the differentiator test, not be assumed
2. AODA applies: clinics are Ontario providers of goods/services → customer-service obligations (accessible feedback process, accessible formats and communication supports on request); larger organizations also have web-content obligations (WCAG 2.0 AA under the IASR) — so clinics will expect the booking page to be accessible; states which obligations fall on the clinic vs the vendor and flags uncertainty for a lawyer
3. Patients are often older adults and people with temporary or permanent mobility, vision or cognitive limits → large targets, text scaling, screen-reader support, no time-limited holds without extension, plain language, accessible authentication (no CAPTCHA puzzles) — WCAG 2.2 AA as floor
4. Accessibility as a possible differentiator for this audience (e.g. accessible, senior-friendly booking page + accessibility statement/ACR clinics can show) — tested with the 5-question test, not just asserted
5. Health information in intake forms → Ontario health privacy (PHIPA) and PIPEDA; data residency questions; card deposits need a payment provider (no raw card data)
6. Feasibility: owner in a different country and time zone selling to Ontario clinics at 12 h/week; 30 clinics in 9 months ambitious; suggests a smaller wedge and a pilot with a few clinics
7. CLARITY questions include launch-market legal applicability and clinic size
